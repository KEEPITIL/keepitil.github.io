AOW2 is the free sequel to Age of War, built into the KEEPITIL apps hub. If the first game was a perfect single skirmish — you, the enemy, one lane, five ages — the sequel is the war that skirmish was always part of. Bigger armies fill the field, new ages extend the tech ladder past where the original topped out, and a world-crusade layer wraps the whole thing in a campaign where each victory pushes your front line further across the map. It's the same instantly-readable core, scaled into something you can actually sink an evening into.

What's new: the war got a map

The headline addition is the world crusade. Instead of one match that ends and resets, AOW2 gives you a campaign map to conquer — a sequence of battles where territory carries forward and each front feels like a step in a larger march rather than a disconnected round. Winning isn't just "I beat this level," it's "I took that region and now the next one's in range." That structure gives the moment-to-moment lane combat a reason to matter beyond the current screen.

On top of the crusade sit missions — objective-driven battles that ask for more than "survive and push." Sometimes it's a defense you have to hold under pressure, sometimes it's a rush against a clock, sometimes it's a specific army composition problem to solve. They break up the rhythm and keep the same base mechanics feeling fresh across dozens of fights.

And when you've had enough structure, there's endless war — the pure, open-ended mode where the enemy just keeps coming and the only question is how deep into the ages you can climb before the line finally breaks. It's the high-score chaser for people who play the campaign and then want to see exactly where their ceiling is.

Who it's for

AOW2 is for the player who finished Age of War and wanted somewhere to put all that muscle memory. If the first game taught you the tempo — when to spend, when to bank, when to leap an age — the sequel is where that instinct pays off across a campaign that remembers your wins. It's for the strategy-game brain that likes a map to fill in, a difficulty curve that respects that you already know the basics, and modes that let you choose your own commitment: a quick mission on a break, or a long crusade run when you've got the evening.

It's also, like everything on the apps hub, for the curious first-timer. You don't need to have played the original — the game explains itself in the first fight, and the sequel's extra depth reveals itself at exactly the pace you're ready for it.

Built to be replayed. Between the crusade map, the mission list, and endless war, there's always a next thing to attempt. It's the kind of game you finish and then immediately restart on a harder line, chasing a cleaner run.
