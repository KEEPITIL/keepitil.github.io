crooki3

Lake Elsinore to the Dance Floor

Lake Elsinore, CA
House / Tech House / Latin House / Techno
KEEPITIL Artist

Lake Elsinore sits 60 miles from LA and 40 miles from San Diego — which means crooki3 learned early that you have to travel for the music, or bring the music to you. This versatile DJ built a sound across House, Tech House, Latin House, and Techno that can hold a room regardless of what that room expects. The genre blend reflects California's cultural mix — the Latin heritage that runs through the Inland Empire, the club culture influences that flow in from both major cities, and the independent creative spirit that defines what the Riverside underground is becoming. The dance floor has been listening.

"Every genre I play has a conversation with the others. That dialogue is the set."
THE STORY
What was it like growing up in Riverside County and developing a passion for House music?
Lake Elsinore is a small city in the western end of Riverside County, and growing up there you're in this interesting middle ground between LA and San Diego that doesn't quite belong to either city's scene. For someone who loves music, that geographic position is actually an advantage in some ways — you get exposed to both cities' scenes without being completely absorbed into either one. House music found me through family — Latin music was always in the house, and the rhythmic connection between traditional Latin music and House is genuinely deep once you start listening for it. The groove, the percussion, the social function of the music as something that brings people together: all of that was already in my DNA before I discovered House music by name.
How does Latin House connect to your identity as a DJ and as a person?
Latin House is the genre where my cultural identity and my musical identity feel most integrated. It's music that carries Latin rhythms and sensibilities into a dance floor context that's also deeply connected to the traditions of House — and House itself has Latin and Black American roots that often get erased in contemporary discussions of the genre. When I play Latin House, I'm honoring multiple heritages simultaneously: the Latin music that I grew up with at home and in community, the House music tradition that I came to love as an electronic music practitioner, and the specific synthesis that artists like Louie Vega have made their life's work. That connection feels personal in a way that no other genre in my repertoire does.
Who are the artists and producers that shaped how you think about House and Tech House?
Fisher was a big early influence on my understanding of how Tech House functions in a live room — the track selection is so smart and the energy management so precise that watching how a Fisher set is built is almost like a masterclass in crowd psychology. Chris Lake showed me that Tech House could have real musicality and production sophistication alongside the functional club elements, that you don't have to sacrifice artistic depth to make music that works in a festival context. Louie Vega is the Latin House foundation — his understanding of how to blend Latin percussion and structure with House music's groove has been studied and will continue to be studied. DJ Snake broadened my sense of how hybrid sounds can reach massive audiences without losing their underground credibility.
When and how did Techno enter your world alongside House?
Techno came in as a contrast to what I was already doing with House and Tech House — I started seeking out harder, darker music as a way of expanding the emotional range of what I could do in a set. The industrial, hypnotic quality of Techno is so different from the groove-forward, body-oriented energy of House that having both in your toolkit gives you an enormous range of choices depending on the room and the moment. The first time I played a genuine Techno track after a House section and felt the energy in the room shift into something more intense and focused, I understood that the contrast itself was an artistic tool. Techno gives me access to a different register of experience that House alone can't provide.
THE CRAFT
How do you navigate between House, Tech House, Latin House, and Techno in a single set without losing cohesion?
The through-line for me is always the groove — every genre I play has a relationship to rhythm and movement that, when understood properly, can connect across genre boundaries. House and Latin House share the most DNA and move between each other naturally. Tech House sits at the intersection of House energy and Techno structure, which makes it a natural bridge genre that can face either direction. Techno is the most distinct departure, so I use it strategically rather than constantly — it's a color I reach for at specific moments in the set to create a shift in intensity that pure House energy can't quite produce. The test I apply to every transition is always: does the emotion continue to make sense, even if the genre changes?
What makes Lake Elsinore an interesting place to develop as a DJ, despite its distance from the major club markets?
The distance forces a particular kind of artistic self-sufficiency. When you can't rely on the ambient energy of a big city scene to carry you along, you have to develop your own sense of what you're doing and why before you take it into the world. I spent a lot of time playing for small groups and practicing in spaces where there was no one to impress, which meant I was developing my technical skills and my taste for their own sake rather than for external validation. The Lake Elsinore music community is smaller than what you'd find in a major city, but it's genuine — when people come to shows here, they're coming because they actually love the music, not because it's socially convenient to be there.
How does your production work feed into your DJing and vice versa?
Production and DJing exist in a constant feedback loop for me. When I'm producing, I'm thinking about how a track will function in a set — what it needs from the tracks on either side of it, where in a night it belongs, what emotion it's designed to serve. And when I'm DJing, I'm constantly having insights about structure and dynamics that I take back to the studio. The act of hearing how a crowd responds to different musical choices in real time is the most honest A/B testing you can do as a producer. I try to put out original tracks that represent the same genre-fluidity I bring to my sets — House with Latin flavors, Tech House with a bit of darkness, and the occasional Techno piece that pushes into harder territory.
THE SCENE
What's the Riverside/IE underground community like right now, and what's being built there?
The Riverside and Inland Empire underground is one of the most exciting developing scenes in Southern California right now, and I say that as someone who lives it rather than observes it from outside. There's a generation of promoters and artists who are committed to building real infrastructure — consistent events, loyal audiences, high-quality bookings — in a region that's often treated as an afterthought compared to LA and San Diego. The diversity of the IE is also reflected in the music: you hear more Latin influence in underground House music here than you do in coastal California scenes, which I think is a genuine cultural contribution to the broader regional electronic music story. The scene is growing, it's serious, and it's developing its own identity.
How has being part of KEEPITIL's network affected your development and visibility?
KEEPITIL has connected me with artists across Southern California who are doing interesting things in underground electronic music that I wouldn't necessarily have crossed paths with otherwise. The network effect of being part of a collective that's actively documenting and building the California underground means that what you're doing gets a context and an audience that's larger than what any individual artist can build alone. For someone coming from Lake Elsinore, that kind of network is genuinely valuable because it extends your reach past your immediate geographic community. I think what KEEPITIL is doing for the underground scene in California is important — creating visibility and community for artists who might otherwise be invisible to each other and to the wider audience.
Where does crooki3 go from here?
More shows across Southern California — both in the IE/Riverside market and in LA, OC, and San Diego — and more original releases that capture the Latin House / Tech House / Techno synthesis that I'm developing. I want to be part of building the IE underground into something that's recognized alongside the other California scenes as having its own distinct voice, and I think that happens through consistent presence, quality output, and genuine investment in the community. The longer-term goal is to have a body of work and a touring presence that allows me to take this sound to other cities and countries, because I genuinely believe that the combination of California cultural diversity and underground electronic sensibility is something that translates internationally. We're building something real here.
