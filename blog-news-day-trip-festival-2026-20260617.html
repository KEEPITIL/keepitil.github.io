Day Trip Drops Anchor Again: House & Techno Take the Queen Mary June 27–28

KEEPITIL Desk June 17, 2026 Long Beach · SoCal

Ten days out, and the SoCal house-and-techno calendar already has its centerpiece. Day Trip Festival returns to the Queen Mary Waterfront in Long Beach on June 27 and 28, 2026 — two days of house, disco and techno on the water, 21+ all weekend, with a brand-new 11,000-capacity amphitheater added to the grounds. For a scene that lives in warehouses and after-hours rooms, it's a rare daytime moment where the underground and the festival circuit shake hands.

Produced under the Insomniac and Day Trip banner, the 2026 edition leans into the sound that's been driving OC and LA dance floors for the past few years: groove-first house, big-room tech and proper techno, with a disco-tinged streak running underneath. Doors run 1 p.m. to midnight each day, so this is sunshine-into-sunset rave energy rather than a 5 a.m. warehouse grind — a different texture, same roots.

The Lineup

The bill is stacked and deliberately diverse. Cloonee, Odd Mob and GRAMMY-nominated DJ Gordo anchor the top of the card, but the real talking points are the curveballs: a debut back-to-back from Chicago house royalty Green Velvet and UK selector Josh Baker, a golden-hour set from Purple Disco Machine, and a SIDEPIECE B2B Westend pairing built for the tent. Add ChaseWest and it's murph on the house side, and the techno contingent — Adam Beyer, Loco Dice, Lilly Palmer, HI-LO, Sidney Charles — gives the heads plenty to chase. Organizers even folded in DJ sets from RuPaul and Rebecca Black, a wink that this thing is as much a party as a purist's pilgrimage. Fifty-plus names round out the two days.

"A daytime festival on the water is a different animal than a 2 a.m. warehouse — but the names on this bill are the same ones holding it down across the OC and LA underground."

Why It Matters For The Underground

Day Trip has quietly become one of the most important bridges between SoCal's grassroots scene and the larger industry. The artists headlining the Queen Mary are, in many cases, the same producers whose tracks are getting hammered in Anaheim warehouses and Hollywood back rooms. A festival of this scale platforming Green Velvet, Loco Dice and a wave of rising house and techno talent is validation that the sound coming out of Southern California's underground isn't niche anymore — it's the main event. The new Long Beach Amphitheater stage signals the festival is scaling up, not coasting.
