KINGPINS

OC Underground Royalty

Orange County, CA
Underground Electronic
KEEPITIL Artist

Some names announce themselves. KINGPINS doesn't do it arrogantly — they do it accurately. Operating in Orange County's underground scene with a commanding presence and an energy that fills whatever room they're in, KINGPINS has built the kind of reputation that the name implies: earned through consistency, respected by peers, and recognized by the kind of underground audience that doesn't give respect cheaply. There's a difference between commanding a room and demanding a room's attention, and KINGPINS has always been firmly in the former category — the authority comes through the music and the energy rather than through posturing or self-promotion. In the OC underground, where reputations are built slowly and genuine credibility is the only currency that matters long-term, KINGPINS has accumulated something that can't be faked or bought: a track record of delivering real, memorable experiences to audiences who knew exactly what quality looked like because they'd seen a lot of the alternative. The crown is earned every set, and KINGPINS keeps earning it.

"The crown comes from the crowd, not the title. You earn it every set."
THE STORY
What does the name KINGPINS represent — where does it come from?
The name is about earned authority rather than claimed authority. In the underground, nobody gives you a crown — you build a reputation through the work, and the name KINGPINS was a commitment to that standard from the beginning. It's not a statement about being above anyone else in the scene; it's a statement about the level of intention and quality we bring to everything we do. The kingpin in any operation is the central, load-bearing figure — the one that everything else depends on running right — and that's how we think about our role in the sets we play: as the thing that makes the night work, the element that the audience will measure the event by. That's a responsibility we take seriously.
How did you build your reputation in OC's underground — what did that process look like?
It was a long process of consistent presence and consistent quality. In the OC underground, the reputation economy works slowly and honestly — people talk to each other, promoters hear from the community, and over time a picture of who reliably delivers and who doesn't starts to form. We showed up to every opportunity, treated every event like it mattered regardless of how many people were there, and made sure that anyone who saw us play once wanted to see us again. Word of mouth in a tight-knit underground scene is the most powerful promotion there is, and the only way to generate the right kind is to actually be what people say you are when they recommend you to their friends.
What does OC underground culture mean to you personally — why does it matter?
OC's underground represents something genuine in a county that often gets flattened into a stereotype of wealth and superficiality. There are real communities here with real cultural life that has nothing to do with the OC of TV shows and tourist perceptions, and the underground music scene is one of the most vibrant expressions of that. Being part of that scene matters because it's one of the places where OC's actual culture gets to exist and breathe — not as a backdrop for something else, but as the thing itself. The underground community here is made up of people who work hard all week and then come to these events because they need something real, and meeting that need feels like meaningful work rather than just entertainment.
What broader electronic music influences have shaped the KINGPINS sound and approach?
The influences run from the foundational rave culture of the late 1980s and early 1990s through to contemporary underground electronic. We're deeply connected to the history of the music — the way that Chicago and Detroit built the original templates for what electronic music could be as a communal, dance-floor-oriented experience — and we try to honor that history while operating in the specific context of OC's current underground scene. Beyond the historical foundations, we draw from across the electronic spectrum: techno, house, drum and bass, and hybrid forms that don't fit neatly into any category. The through line is always the focus on energy and impact rather than genre correctness.
THE CRAFT
How do you approach your sets — what is the philosophy behind a KINGPINS performance?
The philosophy starts from the premise that a set is a conversation with the room, not a performance for the room. We're not presenting something at an audience — we're responding to the collective energy in the space, feeding it back amplified and directed. That means the planned set is always a starting point rather than a fixed script, and the skill is in knowing when to follow the plan and when to throw it out because the room is telling you something different. The commanding energy that people associate with KINGPINS sets comes from that responsiveness — it feels authoritative because it's genuinely connected to what the room needs rather than being imposed on it from outside.
What technical elements are most important to your sound and style?
Precision in the technical execution is fundamental to being able to trust your instincts in the creative moment. If I'm worried about whether a transition is going to work technically, I'm not paying attention to the room — so the technical stuff has to be automatic. Beyond the baseline technical proficiency, the elements that most define our sound are in the selection: the specific tracks we use, the way we sequence them, and the moments we choose to build or release tension. The curation is as much a craft element as the mixing, and we put serious time into understanding what's available and what works in our specific context, with our specific audiences, in OC's specific underground environment.
How do you maintain energy and quality across multiple appearances and different types of events?
Consistency is honestly the hardest part of doing this long-term — the reputation that KINGPINS has built depends on every set meeting the standard, not just the ones where you feel great and the circumstances are ideal. We work hard on preparation before events to make sure we're bringing fresh material and fresh energy to every show rather than running the same set repeatedly. There's also a discipline element: taking the time between shows to listen carefully to what's happening in the music, to find new material, and to think about what we want to do differently. The KINGPINS reputation was built on consistency, and maintaining that consistency is the ongoing work that makes the reputation mean something.
What is your vision for what you want to build in OC's underground scene?
The vision is for OC's underground to have the kind of depth and infrastructure that makes it a destination rather than just a local scene — somewhere that people from across Southern California and beyond would travel to for the specific experience of what's happening here. That requires the whole ecosystem to grow: better events, stronger artist development, more documentation of what the scene is doing, and more collaboration between the different corners of OC's underground. KINGPINS wants to be part of that infrastructure-building, not just as performers but as community members who help connect people and elevate the overall quality and visibility of what OC's underground is doing. The scene deserves to be known.
THE SCENE
What makes the OC underground audience distinctive compared to other Southern California scenes?
OC's underground audience is experienced and discerning in ways that sometimes surprise people who haven't spent time in the scene. Because the scene is relatively small and doesn't have a constant influx of newcomers the way LA does, the regular community has been attending events for years and has a genuine depth of knowledge about the music. That means you can't coast on a basic, predictable set — the audience knows the difference between someone who is genuinely working and someone who is going through the motions. That high standard is demanding to perform for, but it's also exactly the right environment for pushing yourself to do better work, because the appreciation you get when you actually land something exceptional is completely authentic.
What role does community play in what KINGPINS does — how do you think about your relationship to the broader OC underground?
Community is everything. The name KINGPINS only means something in the context of a community that confers that kind of recognition — there's no authority in a vacuum. We think of what we do as fundamentally in service to the community that has supported us, which means we prioritize showing up for other artists' events, helping connect people across the scene, and contributing to the collective culture rather than just extracting from it. The OC underground is stronger when its established artists invest in its development rather than just taking bookings, and that's a responsibility we feel seriously. The scene gave us something, and building it for the people coming up behind us is the return on that investment.
What does the future of OC underground electronic music look like from where you're standing?
I see the OC underground going through a genuinely exciting period of development — there's more documentation happening, more connection between different corners of the scene, and a growing sense that OC has a real underground identity worth developing and protecting. KEEPITIL is part of that, and there are other things happening at the promoter and venue level that suggest the infrastructure is maturing. The artists coming up right now are serious and talented, and the scene that developed over the past decade is strong enough to support real growth without losing the values that made it worth caring about. The future looks like a scene that's both more visible and truer to itself — which is the ideal outcome and not an easy balance to strike.
