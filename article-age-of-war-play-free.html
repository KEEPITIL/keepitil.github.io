The setup is old-school in the best way: you own a base, the enemy owns a base, and the ground between you is a single lane where everything gets decided. You spawn units, they march right, the enemy spawns units that march left, and the two waves crash in the middle. Win the exchanges, push the line, and take down their fort before they take down yours. That's the game. What makes it stick is everything layered on top of that clean spine.

Five ages, one escalating arms race

The hook that gives the game its name is evolution. You don't stay in the Stone Age tossing clubs the whole match. As you earn experience from kills, you unlock the next age — and the whole texture of your army changes with it. Stone clubs give way to swords and spears, then to gunpowder, then to modern rifles, then to a future age where your foot soldiers are carrying energy weapons and your defensive turrets are firing things that didn't exist a minute ago.

The genius of it is the pressure it creates. Evolving costs experience you could have spent on units right now, so every match becomes a running gamble: do you spend to hold the line this second, or bank it to leap an age ahead and out-tech the enemy in thirty seconds? Rush too hard and you stall out in an early age while a smarter opponent's future-tech walks through you. Turtle too long and you get overrun before your upgrade ever lands. The five-age arc turns a simple lane battle into a real decision every few seconds.
