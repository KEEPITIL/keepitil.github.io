Nobody in the underground got into this for the money. But rent in SoCal doesn't care about your art, and the difference between quitting and keeping going is usually a few hundred dollars a month.

This is the stack we actually recommend — tools that either pay you directly, pay you back over time, or stop you from burning money you didn't need to spend.

01. DistroKid — keep 100% of your royalties

If you're still sitting on finished tracks because you don't know how to get them on Spotify and Apple Music, this is the move. DistroKid charges a flat yearly fee and takes zero percent of your streaming royalties — upload unlimited tracks, keep everything they earn. For a bedroom producer putting out a track a month, the math beats every royalty-cut distributor by a mile.

Organizers: Posh is the ticketing platform half the SoCal underground already runs on, and it's built for exactly the shows KEEPITIL lists — warehouse parties, club takeovers, backyard sets. Clean guest lists, fast payouts, no enterprise-tier nonsense.

If you've been collecting cash at the door and losing track of who's coming, moving to real ticketing is the single biggest professionalization step you can take — promoters and venues take you more seriously when your numbers are real.

04. Gear that pays you back by not failing

The rest of the stack is defensive: gear that stops you losing money, sets, or hearing.

Loop Experience 2 earplugs — the most-bought item in the KEEPITIL shop, and for good reason. High-fidelity filtering means you hear the mix, not mud, and your ears still work at 30. If you play or attend weekly, hearing protection isn't optional — tinnitus is permanent, earplugs are twenty bucks.

A real power bank — dead phone means no set recordings, no contacts exchanged, no ride home. A 10,000mAh bank is the cheapest insurance in music.

Cable organization — every DJ who's lost an RCA mid-set knows. A $12 cable bag saves a $200 gig.

The principle

Every dollar here goes one of two directions: into infrastructure that makes your work findable and bookable (distribution, ticketing), or into protection for the gear and body you perform with. That's the whole philosophy. No course-selling, no dropshipping, no "six figures with AI" — just the boring, compounding stuff that keeps independent artists independent.
