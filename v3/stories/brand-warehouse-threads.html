Warehouse Threads is a Downtown Los Angeles streetwear brand built explicitly for the underground electronic music community — heavy fabrics, minimal graphics, and a design philosophy informed by warehouse aesthetics rather than streetwear trends. Founded by a DTLA event photographer who got tired of wearing clothes that weren't built for all-night events.

The Warehouse Threads aesthetic is deliberately anti-logo, anti-trend, and pro-function. Heavy cotton hoodies that warm you between sets in cold warehouse spaces. Pants with pockets that hold cards and keys without bouncing when you dance. T-shirts that look better after a year of use than they do new. The brand is built for the long haul.

DTLA is where the warehouse scene lives in LA, and we're embedded in it. Our HQ is walking distance from several of the venues where the events we care about happen. That proximity isn't accidental — we want to be part of the community we're making clothes for, not a brand that observes it from outside.

Spending years photographing events means I know exactly what people look for in practical terms — what fabrics move well, what silhouettes look good under event lighting, what holds up through a 10-hour night of dancing and crowd movement. That observation went directly into every design decision.
