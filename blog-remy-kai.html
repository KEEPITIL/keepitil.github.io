REMY KAI

UKG, Bassline and Future Sounds from the OC

📍 Irvine, CA 🎵 UKG / Bassline / Bass House ⭐ KEEPITIL Artist

Remy Kai has emerged as one of the most exciting UKG-influenced voices in the OC underground — sets that pull from UK Garage's classic era and its contemporary revivals, combined with bassline and the harder end of bass house. Irvine-based, OC committed, and increasingly present at events alongside artists like RAB3L who share the UKG passion.

"UKG is the most rhythmically sophisticated dance music ever made and it's been criminally underexposed in America."
THE STORY
Irvine is planned communities and tech money — a very specific California aesthetic. How does that environment connect to UK Garage?
Irvine is exactly the kind of place that makes you want to find music that feels real, because the environment is so curated and controlled. UKG is music that came from Black British working-class communities pushing back against cultural invisibility — it's as far from Irvine as you can get, and maybe that contrast is part of why it resonates so much for me. I found it through online rabbit holes at 16 and it reoriented everything.
How did you connect with the OC underground scene and specifically with RAB3L?
Through shows, honestly. The OC underground is small enough that if you go to events consistently and show genuine interest, you meet everyone. RAB3L was playing a set at a show I attended early in my DJing journey and I introduced myself afterward. We've shared bills since then and both of us are trying to build awareness of UKG in a market that doesn't always know what to do with it.
THE CRAFT
What distinguishes bassline from UKG for listeners who might not know the difference?
Bassline is a UK subgenre that developed in Sheffield in the early 2000s — it has a harder, more aggressive quality than classic London UKG, with a very specific bass pattern and a faster tempo. UKG is more groove-forward, more melodic, more influenced by R&B and soul. When I'm mixing bassline into a UKG set, I'm using the energy difference to create moments of lift and release. They complement each other perfectly when sequenced right.
What's the future you're building toward as an artist?
More original productions in the UKG space, more shows, and hopefully contributing to building a genuine UKG community in SoCal rather than it just being a niche interest for a few of us. The music deserves a bigger audience here.
