Skyline Gave LA's Underground a Mainstage — And the Locals Stole the Weekend

KEEPITIL Desk June 20, 2026 DTLA Arts District

Insomniac moved Skyline Festival to an Arts District warehouse this year and did something most big promoters won't: it handed an entire stage to LA's own. The Arts District stage — built inside a vast warehouse at Ace*Mission Studios on the edge of the LA river — was reserved for the city's homegrown DJs. By most accounts coming out of the weekend, those locals didn't just hold their own. They ran it.

Skyline has been nomadic since it launched in 2021, bouncing from Skylight ROW to Exposition Park to Grand Park before landing in 2026 at a repurposed Budweiser distribution complex on the edge of the Arts District — the same corridor where LA's rave underground throws illicit parties every weekend. This year the festival grew to four stages, and the boldest of them was the one with no international headliners on it at all.

Local Heroes, Big Sound

The Arts District lineup read like a who's-who of the LA grind: Baile World founder CQUESTT sending the warehouse into Jersey-club and ghetto-house overdrive, Signal Underground's Mez Monty locking into sultry deep house, Gyration Station's DJ Warning torching the room with East-to-West Coast chaos, and LA native Star Eyes (Vivian Host) closing Sunday at warp speed. These are the artists who keep dance music alive at ground level — the tastemakers who rarely get the renown handed to the touring DJs who fly in on the strength of that grassroots grind.

"To dedicate an entire stage of a two-day festival to shining a light on L.A.'s underground DJs is a huge 'look' — it tells festivalgoers, promoters, booking agents and managers that we are important and worth paying attention to." — Star Eyes

That's the whole point, and it's why this matters past one weekend. When a promoter the size of Insomniac — a company that started in LA warehouses in 1993 before EDC made it a global name — puts its grassroots scene on a real stage with a real soundsystem, it tells every bedroom producer and small-room promoter in OC and LA that the ceiling is higher than they thought. CQUESTT put it plainly: she hopes other festivals "take notes" and give more opportunities to the real culture movers, "that may not have 100,000 followers, but are the real tastemakers."

The energy carried past the gates. CQUESTT recently launched an LA promoter fund through her Baile World party — a dollar from every ticket goes to a local from an underrepresented background to throw their own night. That's the model: platform the locals, then reinvest in the next ones. A festival stage is a long way from an illegal warehouse, but for one weekend the Arts District belonged to the people who built the underground — and they made the case that they always deserved it.
