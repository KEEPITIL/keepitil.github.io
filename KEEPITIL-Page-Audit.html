How the brain works today

Read from the live schema on 2026-08-13, not from documentation. The distinction below is the single most important architectural fact about the current system.

The KEEPITIL brain is not a model. It is a Postgres full-text matcher over a hand-curated answer bank. agent_knowledge.search is a tsvector — all 1,180 rows populated. A question is matched against it lexically and the stored answer_short / answer_full is returned verbatim. No inference happens. No tokens are spent. Nothing is generated at answer time.
TableRowsWhat it actually is
agent_knowledge1,180The central bank. topic, keywords, answer_short, answer_full, tier, confidence, genre_lane, department, related_agents, review_at, search tsvector. 5 tiers: CS, L1, L2, L3, public. Average confidence 0.52 — the bank rates itself barely better than a coin flip.
agent_brain1,615A join table, not a second brain: agent_slug → knowledge_id with relevance and source. Each of the 14 agents holds a weighted view onto the one shared bank. 1,615 links over 1,180 items ≈ 1.4 agents per fact.
agent_roundtable1,503spark_agent, spark_topic → responder_agent, responder_topic, line. Agents writing dialogue lines at each other's topics. This table is the "talking to itself" number made literal.
agent_handoffs112from_agent → to_agent + question + reason. Routing when one lane can't answer.
agent_questions159Unanswered questions — the growth queue.
knowledge_suggestions30Proposed additions awaiting review. The approval gate works.
chat_answer_log17Answers actually delivered to humans.
Why this design is better than it sounds. A tsvector matcher is deterministic, free, auditable and cannot hallucinate — it can only return text a human approved. Under Zero Cost Mode that is a feature, not a limitation. The weakness is equally clear: it only answers questions phrased close to stored keywords, it cannot synthesise across two facts, and review_at exists but 0 rows are currently due, so nothing is being re-checked for staleness.

Brain → NEXUS: the merge path

Direction of travel matters. NEXUS is the brain; KEEPITIL's bank is one high-quality corpus feeding it — not a competing brain to be preserved.

#StepWhat happens, and why in this order
1Bind, don't migrateNEXUS reaches keepitil-prod through an explicit capability, read-only. The 1,180 rows stay where they are. Copying them creates two divergent banks and no way to tell which is right.
2Map to NEXUS memoryagent_knowledge → NEXUS memories. The shapes already correspond: confidence→confidence, tier→scope, genre_lane/department→tags, review_at→supersession review. app_id = "keepitil" on every row so cross-app isolation holds from the first insert.
3Keep lexical, add vectorNEXUS's embedding vector(384) column is deliberately null today. Populate it alongside the tsvector, never instead of it. Lexical retrieval is the free, deterministic floor; embeddings are the recall improvement layered on top. Losing the floor to gain recall is a bad trade under Zero Cost Mode.
4Agents become views, not brainsagent_brain is already a relevance-weighted view onto a shared bank — which is exactly the NEXUS model. This is the piece that ports cleanly and should not be redesigned. The 14 genre lanes become NEXUS scopes.
5Route through the capability layeragent_handoffs becomes NEXUS capability routing; knowledge_suggestions becomes the approval queue. Both already exist in NEXUS — the KEEPITIL versions retire rather than merge.
6Answer-time fallbackMatch lexically first; only on a miss escalate to inference. That preserves 17→N growth at near-zero marginal cost and keeps the audit trail intact.
Two things to decide before any of this runs.
1. Average confidence is 0.52. Importing 1,180 rows at coin-flip confidence into the brain that will govern every app is how a weak corpus becomes an authoritative one. Gate the import on a confidence floor, or re-score first.
2. agent_roundtable (1,503 rows) is agent-generated dialogue, not verified knowledge. It must not enter NEXUS memory. It is the largest table after agent_brain and the easiest to sweep in by accident — model output re-ingested as fact is the classic way a knowledge base poisons itself.

Roadmap — what keepitil.com / the app actually is

One sentence: KEEPITIL is a discovery and identity layer for creators. Commerce is downstream of discovery, not the product.
Correction, 2026-08-13. An earlier version of this section framed KEEPITIL as "a discovery and identity layer for underground event culture." That was wrong and it narrowed the product to its origin. EDM / SoCal / underground is the beachhead — the scene the graph was seeded in — not the scope. The standing directive is explicit: a creator ecosystem across all genres and industries, worldwide. Framing matters here because it decides what gets built: a scene aggregator optimises for one city's event calendar, whereas a creator layer optimises for the entity graph — creators, their work, their audiences, their commerce — with events as one surface among several.

What the re-framing changes in practice. Ordering the table by the profile rather than the event matches the graph directive — think objects, not pages. It also reprioritises P1: the profile SEO blocker stops being a housekeeping item and becomes the central one, because durable, indexed creator pages are the product. Six problems are genuinely solved in software and none are yet solved for a real person, because the funnel has no one in it. Every remaining engineering task — QR camera, push, offline tickets — is downstream of having ticket buyers, which has not happened once.
