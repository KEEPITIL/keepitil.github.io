Brownies & Lemonade: How a College Party Became LA's Biggest Electronic Brand

KEEPITIL Desk June 17, 2026 Los Angeles · Promoter Spotlight

In 2012, a group of UCLA students started throwing parties. Not extraordinary parties — just good ones, with good music, promoted through social media with a level of creativity and aesthetic consistency that stood apart from the average college event. Thirteen years later, Brownies & Lemonade is one of the most influential independent live music brands in the United States: a company that has sold out the Shrine Auditorium, launched before-known artists into mainstream conversations, and redefined what independent concert promotion in LA can look like.

The Early Years

Brownies & Lemonade was founded by a group of friends at UCLA in 2012. The name — intentionally absurd, memorably distinctive — reflected the brand's early approach: take the concert experience seriously, but don't take yourself too seriously. Early events were small enough to feel like parties among friends, with music selections that favored emerging internet-native artists over established touring acts.

The brand's Instagram presence was central to its growth from the beginning. B&L understood early that the aesthetic of an event — the flyers, the branding, the way it looks on social — is inseparable from the event itself. Every B&L event communicated a consistent visual identity: bright colors, bold typography, a specific kind of LA cool that felt both accessible and aspirational.

The Booking Philosophy That Changed LA

Brownies & Lemonade made its reputation by booking artists before they were obvious choices. The promoter's team developed a genuine A&R sensibility — identifying artists who were gaining traction online before they had mainstream radio play or festival slots — and betting on them early.

Early B&L shows included artists who went on to become some of the most significant names in contemporary pop and electronic music. The fact that the company was willing to take those bets — and had the taste and infrastructure to do it successfully — built a level of audience trust that most concert brands never achieve. When Brownies & Lemonade announces a new act, the people who follow them closely take it seriously as a quality signal.

Growth Without Selling Out

The defining challenge for any successful independent music brand is scaling without losing the identity that made it successful. Brownies & Lemonade has navigated this more successfully than most: as the events have grown in scale, the curatorial sensibility has remained sharp. The company has expanded to other markets (New York, Austin, Miami) while maintaining the LA identity at its core.

The secret may be the founding team's continued involvement in the day-to-day creative decisions. Unlike major concert corporations that scale through management layers and standardized booking approaches, B&L maintains a culture where the artistic direction is still driven by people with genuine investment in the music.

What B&L Means for the Underground

Brownies & Lemonade occupies an interesting position in the LA music ecosystem: too big to be truly underground, too independent and taste-driven to be comfortably corporate. For artists working the California circuit, that middle position is valuable. A B&L booking is a credibility marker that crosses the divide between underground and mainstream in ways that few other brands can claim.

For KEEPITIL artists and promoters, B&L represents what's possible when you build a brand with genuine aesthetic conviction and let the quality of the curation speak for itself over a long period. The path from small parties to major venue shows isn't automatic — but it is replicable, if the fundamentals are right.
