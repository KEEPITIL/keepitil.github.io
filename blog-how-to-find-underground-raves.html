How to Find Underground Raves in LA & OC

KEEPITIL Desk June 18, 2026 Tips & Guides · OC & LA Underground

Underground raves don't advertise on billboards. That's intentional — and it's part of what makes them worth finding. Here's how to actually get into the underground scene in LA and OC without wandering into the wrong event.

Start With The Right Instagram Accounts

Instagram is the primary flyer distribution channel for underground events in SoCal. Follow local organizers, artists, and collectives rather than general event discovery pages. Accounts like @keepitil curate verified underground events across OC and LA — that's exactly what we're built for. When a promoter you follow reposts an event, that endorsement is meaningful in a small scene where reputation matters.

Look for accounts with 500–5,000 followers posting actual event flyers with real venue information. Be skeptical of anything promising massive lineups with suspiciously cheap tickets — underground events stay underground partly through controlled access.

Build Actual Scene Relationships

The underground scene in LA and OC is not anonymous. Regulars at events know each other. DJs show up to each other's gigs. Promoters become friends with their regulars. If you attend events consistently and engage genuinely — not just for content — you'll find yourself getting tipped off to the parties that don't get posted publicly.

Pro TipTalk to the opening DJ. They're almost always a local artist who knows every event happening that month and usually has flyers or contacts to share.

Use These Specific Platforms

Beyond Instagram, the SoCal underground uses a few consistent channels. RA (Resident Advisor) lists legitimate events and has solid event verification. Eventbrite is used by mid-tier promoters for ticketing but rarely captures truly underground events. Facebook Events are still relevant for older promoters who built their audiences before Instagram. Telegram groups run by specific collectives provide advance notice for their events — getting added requires genuine community connection.

Know the Difference Between Promoter Types

Not every party called "underground" is underground. Club promoters who book international headliners are running nightlife businesses, not underground events — there's nothing wrong with that, but it's a different experience. True underground events are typically run by collectives or small promoters who are themselves part of the music community, with lineups built around local talent and handpicked guests rather than algorithm-determined booking.

Safe Practices

Warehouse and underground venues operate outside normal nightclub safety regulations. Go with people you trust. Tell someone where you're going before you leave. Look up the address before you go — not all venues listed are active or safe. Bring water and cash. Know where the exits are. The underground scene at its best is a genuine community, but like any scene, it requires you to look out for yourself and others.
