How we picked these (and why we show the payouts)

1. DistroKid — for artists releasing music

If you're a producer or DJ sitting on finished tracks, DistroKid is the standard move: unlimited uploads to Spotify, Apple Music, TikTok, and the rest of the major stores for one flat annual fee, and you keep 100% of your royalties.[1] Most of the SoCal artists on our directory already distribute through it. Signing up through our link gets you 7% off your first year — and pays KEEPITIL $10 per referral.

Put your tracks on every major streaming platform and keep all your royalties.

2. Rave Essentials — the gear we'd actually bring to a warehouse

Our Amazon storefront is eight items deep on purpose. No 200-product junk drawer — just the stuff that survives a 10-hour warehouse night: Loop earplugs that let you hear the mix without frying your ears, Liquid I.V. for the morning after, a hydration pack, a 10K power bank, and a few glow pieces. Purchases through the shop pay KEEPITIL a standard Amazon Associates commission (roughly 3–4% on most gear).[2]

Eight curated festival-survival picks — earplugs, hydration, power, glow.

3. FreeCash — ticket money for doing tasks on your phone

FreeCash pays you to test apps and games and answer surveys, with cash-outs to PayPal, crypto, or gift cards.[3] It's not a job and it won't replace one — but knocking out offers during a slow week can genuinely cover a ticket to Friday's show. Signing up through our link pays KEEPITIL up to $12.50 per referral.

Earn cash testing apps, games, and surveys — cash out to PayPal, crypto, or gift cards.

4. Posh — for organizers listing their next event

If you're throwing a show, Posh is the ticketing platform we see the most across the OC/LA underground — clean event pages, built-in text-message marketing to your past attendees, and payouts organizers don't have to chase.[4] Creating your organizer group through our link supports KEEPITIL on qualifying signups, and costs you nothing.

List your event, sell tickets, and market to your crowd — the platform half the scene already uses.
