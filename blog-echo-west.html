ECHO WEST

House Music and Community from Long Beach

📍 Long Beach, CA 🎵 House / Deep House / Soulful House ⭐ KEEPITIL Artist

Echo West brings a soulful, community-centered approach to house music from Long Beach — a city with its own distinct music culture that often gets overlooked between LA and OC. Sets built on joy, groove, and the specific emotional warmth of soulful house, ECHO WEST has become one of the most beloved artists on the Long Beach and South Bay underground circuit.

"House music is joy with roots — and the roots are what make the joy last."
THE STORY
Long Beach has this amazing, diverse musical culture that gets overshadowed by LA — what's the underground scene actually like there?
Long Beach is incredibly musically rich. The hip-hop culture here is serious, there's a strong jazz history, and the underground electronic scene has this specific warmth to it that I think comes from the city's demographics and culture. The house music scene here is more soulful, more R&B-influenced than what you typically find in more techno-oriented scenes. It reflects the city.
Soulful house is a subgenre with deep roots in the Black church and gospel tradition. How do you carry those origins into your sets?
With awareness and reverence. House music was made by Black artists in Black spaces for Black communities, and soulful house is particularly direct in its connection to gospel and soul music. When I'm playing Frankie Knuckles or Larry Heard or contemporary artists who carry that tradition forward, I'm aware of what that music comes from and what it means. The joy in soulful house is an earned joy — it's not superficial positivity, it's the kind of joy that exists alongside hardship.
THE CRAFT
How do you bring new listeners into a music with that kind of depth?
By letting the music speak first. I don't lecture crowds about the history or politics of house music — I play it well and trust that the feeling communicates what words can't. When someone discovers house music through a powerful live set, they often go back and find the history on their own. The experience creates the curiosity.
What does the Long Beach underground need in 2026?
More consistent venues and more cross-pollination with the OC scene to the south. Long Beach is geographically between LA and OC but culturally disconnected from both. Building bridges between those communities — which KEEPITIL's geographic coverage helps with — would strengthen all three.
