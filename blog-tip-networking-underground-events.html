How to Network at Underground Events Without Being Weird

📅 June 9, 2026 ✍ KEEPITIL Editorial 🎵 OC & LA Underground

Every booking relationship, every collaboration, and every gig that leads to another gig in the underground runs through a personal connection. The underground is small enough that relationships compound — being known as someone who shows up, supports the scene, and engages authentically matters more than most people realize.

The Don’ts

Don’t approach DJs immediately after their set to pitch yourself. Don’t hand out USB drives at the venue. Don’t slide into DMs with booking pitches from a personal account with 40 followers. Don’t tell people how good you are — let them hear it.

Networking in the underground is just showing up and being a real person. The rest follows naturally or not at all.

The Dos

Show up early and stay late. Talk to people in line, at the bar, at the merch table. Engage with the event as an experience, not as a networking opportunity. The relationships that convert to bookings start with being a genuine part of the community, not with a pitch.

Approaching Promoters

The best time to approach a promoter is not at their event — they’re working. Connect on Instagram or through mutual friends. Have something to offer (a SoundCloud link, a demo mix) rather than just a request. Make it easy for them to hear your music without requiring effort from them.

The Long Game

Underground scene relationships develop over years. Be consistent, be genuine, support the artists and events you believe in, and the rest follows. There are no shortcuts that work for long.
