VELVET RIOT

UK Bass and Garage from the Los Angeles Underground

📍 Los Angeles, CA 🎵 UK Bass / Garage / Bass House ⭐ KEEPITIL Artist

Velvet Riot has been one of the most distinctive voices in the LA bass music underground since 2022 — bringing UK garage rhythms and bass house weight to a scene that doesn't always know what to do with genuine UKG influence. Her sets convert skeptics because the feeling is undeniable: the shuffle rhythm of garage is one of the most physically compelling things you can do to a dance floor.

"The shuffle doesn't lie — it's the most honest rhythm there is."
THE STORY
You're playing UK Garage in Los Angeles — how does that land with audiences who don't have a reference point for the sound?
Better than you'd expect, because the body knows before the brain labels it. UKG has this specific shuffle that makes people want to move in a way that's different from four-on-the-floor house. When I drop into a proper UKG groove, I watch the room change — people stop nodding their heads on the beat and start moving more fluidly. That physical recognition happens even when people have no cultural reference for what they're hearing.
How did you come to UK Garage specifically rather than the more common bass house route?
An ex sent me a Dusk + Blackdown compilation as a joke — 'you'll hate this' — and I played it for three days straight. The rhythmic sophistication of proper UKG, the vocal culture, the way it sits between R&B and club music — it felt like someone had made exactly the music I'd been looking for without knowing I was looking.
THE CRAFT
What's your approach to mixing UKG with contemporary bass house?
They share a rhythmic skeleton — the swing and syncopation of UKG lives inside the best bass house tracks even if the producers don't acknowledge it. The transitions work when you find tracks where the underlying rhythmic feel is compatible, regardless of what genre label they carry. I think of my sets less as genre sets and more as explorations of swing and syncopation at various tempos.
What do you see as the biggest gap in the LA bass music scene right now?
Genuine rhythmic diversity. A lot of LA bass music is straight four-on-the-floor with sub-bass — which sounds powerful but doesn't make me want to dance the way UKG makes me want to dance. I want to hear more producers working with shuffled rhythms, off-beat kick patterns, the kind of rhythmic complexity that exists in non-European musical traditions. That's the frontier.
