ZENITH

Melodic Techno and Peak-Time Programming from LA

📍 Hollywood, CA 🎵 Melodic Techno / Progressive / Peak-Time ⭐ KEEPITIL Artist

ZENITH has built a following in the Los Angeles underground on the strength of peak-time melodic techno sets that hit with emotional weight as well as physical impact. A regular at rooftop events and mid-size LA venues, ZENITH represents the convergence of melodic sensibility and driving techno energy that defines the current moment in the genre.

"You're not chasing the sound — you're chasing the feeling that the sound creates."
THE STORY
Melodic techno has had a massive mainstream moment — how do you stay connected to underground credibility while working in a sound that's blown up?
By caring more about the music than the positioning. The melodic techno conversation in 2026 has become very focused on a small number of very recognizable producers — Afterlife, Tale Of Us, and their orbit. But there's a whole world of melodic techno that doesn't fit neatly into that aesthetic, and that's where I live. The emotion is the same; the approach is more individual.
What makes a melodic techno track work for you from a DJ perspective?
The melodic element has to serve the drive of the track rather than softening it. The melodic techno tracks that I play at peak time are emotionally open and physically driving simultaneously — they create a kind of hopeful intensity that I don't hear in purely functional techno. When a melody lands in the middle of a peak-time set and the room goes up instead of going quiet, that's when you know it worked.
THE CRAFT
How do you program a set that moves through melodic techno without becoming one-dimensional?
Variation in the texture of the emotion — joyful melodic techno, melancholic melodic techno, aggressive melodic techno, expansive melodic techno. The BPM range might only move five or six points across a set, but the emotional character of each track can be completely different. The structure of the set should feel like a complete emotional journey even if the tempo barely moves.
What's your relationship with the broader LA club scene versus the underground circuit?
I play both and I value both. The bigger club circuit gives you access to production quality and room sizes that the underground can't match. The underground gives you freedom and proximity to the audience that changes how you play. I try not to make enemies of either — they're different experiences of the same music, for different contexts.
