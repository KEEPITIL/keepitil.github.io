The History of House Music: From Chicago to California

KEEPITILJune 15, 2026OC & LA Underground

House music is now a global language — but it started in one specific place, for one specific community, for very specific reasons. Understanding that origin still matters when you're in a room in Orange County at 2AM feeling something you can't quite name.

Chicago, 1977–1985

The Warehouse opened on South Jefferson Street in 1977. Its DJ, Frankie Knuckles, played Philly soul, European disco, and synth-driven dance music for a crowd pushed out of mainstream nightlife. What he did to that music — editing tape, extending breakdowns, layering drum machines — was house before it had a name.

The Chicago Sound Takes Shape

By 1983 the Music Box opened with Ron Hardy — rawer and more experimental. Jesse Saunders pressed the first house record in 1984; Larry Heard's "Can You Feel It" (1986) still sounds current. These tracks circulated as cassettes before anyone released them.

California's Relationship With House

California built its own house culture — LA's soul and funk tradition, Latin rhythms, and the demographics of Southern California shaped a distinct West Coast sound through the 1990s and beyond.
