Goldenvoice Drops Head Trip — A One-Stage EDM Festival on the Coachella Grounds

KEEPITIL Desk June 21, 2026 Empire Polo Club · Indio

Insomniac doesn't own SoCal dance music — and Goldenvoice just made sure everyone knows it. The promoter behind Coachella is launching Head Trip, a one-time-only electronic music festival taking over the Empire Polo Club in Indio on October 10–11. Two days, one stage, no overlapping sets, and a lineup that reads less like a radio chart and more like a record-shop wishlist. For a desert that usually only wakes up for Coachella weekends, this is a statement.

Head Trip continues Goldenvoice's "one-time-only" tradition — the same framing it used for the era-defining one-offs that turned the Polo Grounds into more than a spring festival site. Calvin Harris headlines Saturday and Swedish House Mafia closes Sunday, the big-room names that move a desert crowd. But scan past the top line and the curation tilts straight toward the heads.

A Lineup Built for the Diggers

This is where Head Trip earns the underground's attention. Peggy Gou goes back-to-back with Four Tet. Dom Dolla links with KETTAMA. Fisher and Chris Lake reprise their Under Construction project. The Blessed Madonna and DJ Harvey both get slots, Seth Troxler pairs with Ben Sterling, and Floating Points plays all weekend long on the Sunflower Sound System stage — a nod to the kind of sound-system worship that lives in warehouses, not on festival mainstages. Skrillex rounds out the bass contingent. It's a bill where the b2b is the format, not the gimmick.

"One stage, no clashes, no choosing between two sets you came to see. That's how the underground has always done it — and now Coachella's promoter is doing it in the desert."

The single-stage format is the real flex. No FOMO, no sprinting across a field, no two legends scheduled against each other. Goldenvoice is leaning into a centerpiece dome tent with mycelium paneling and commissioned artwork — a curated, art-forward experience over the sprawling multi-stage arms race. It's a different philosophy from the Insomniac model, and it's one the underground recognizes.

Why does a desert festival matter to the OC and LA underground? Because it shifts the map. When Goldenvoice — the institution that built Coachella — bets on a single-stage, tastemaker-driven dance event, it pulls the culture's center of gravity closer to the people who've been championing this music in small rooms for years. Two-day GA passes start at $399, with VIP and car-camping options for those making the Indio trek. A long way from a Santa Ana warehouse, sure — but the names on this poster are the same ones our DJs grew up studying.
