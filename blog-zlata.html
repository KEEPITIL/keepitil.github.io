ZLATA

On Her Own Terms

Los Angeles, CA
House / Techno / Trance
KEEPITIL Artist

ZLATA arrived at dance music the long way — through a classical foundation as a pianist and singer before turning to production and the booth. The Los Angeles DJ, producer and vocalist has built a fast-rising profile on high-energy house and techno, the founder of her own label, debosh, and an artist who has earned releases on imprints including Insomniac and Femme House. She has played rooms from Sound in Los Angeles to KitKat Club in Berlin and the Ultra stage in Miami, and supported names like Eli Brown, Bart Skils and Reinier Zonneveld. The throughline across all of it is independence: a career built deliberately, on her own terms.

"A classically trained voice, a producer's ear, and a techno engine — all pointed at the peak of the night."

THE STORY
Where does the music begin for ZLATA?
With a piano and a voice. A classical background as a pianist and trained singer gives her an unusual entry point into electronic music — an ear shaped by harmony and melody long before it was shaped by kick drums. That foundation is audible in the way her productions and sets treat melody as a driving force rather than an afterthought, and in the fact that she can contribute her own vocals to a record instead of reaching for a sample. The pivot to production came later, but it was built on a musician's training, not a hobbyist's.
How did the rise happen so quickly?
By treating the craft seriously and refusing to wait for permission. ZLATA spent focused time sharpening her production at a dedicated electronic-music academy, then put the work into the world through her own channels — including the label she founded, debosh. Momentum followed: chart-topping releases, support slots for established techno acts, and bookings at marquee venues. It reads as fast from the outside, but it is the product of deliberate, self-directed building rather than a single viral moment.
What does "on her own terms" actually mean?
It means owning the infrastructure around the music. Founding debosh gives her a release home she controls, and a vocalist-producer who writes and sings her own material is not dependent on anyone else to complete a track. In a scene where artists are often shaped by the labels and managers around them, ZLATA's path has been notably self-authored — the label, the productions, the vocal performances and the bookings all flowing from decisions she makes rather than ones made for her.

THE CRAFT
What does the classical training bring to a techno set?
Structure and tension. Classical music is built on the careful management of expectation — building toward a resolution, withholding it, releasing it at the right moment. Applied to a dance floor, that instinct becomes the ability to architect a set's energy: knowing how to layer a melodic phrase under a driving rhythm, when to let a breakdown breathe, and how to time a peak so it lands as an event rather than an accident. The vocabulary is different, but the underlying skill — controlling how a listener feels over time — is the same one she trained for years.
House, techno, and a Beatport-topping trance record — how do they coexist?
Through a shared love of high energy and melody. ZLATA's core is house and techno, but her range stretches into trance, and her catalog has reached the top of the charts in that lane as well. Rather than splitting these into separate personas, she treats them as points on a single spectrum united by drive and emotional lift. A trance sensibility — big, melodic, euphoric — informs the way she builds even her techno material, which is part of why her sets translate across very different rooms.
What does it mean to be a producer, DJ and vocalist all at once?
Total authorship over the sound. Most artists occupy one or two of those roles; holding all three means a ZLATA record can be written, produced and sung by the same person, and then road-tested in her own sets. That closed loop — studio to booth and back — keeps the music personal and lets her develop a recognizable signature rather than a sound assembled from outside parts. The voice, in particular, is a differentiator: a human, melodic presence inside music that is often purely instrumental.
What is debosh built to do?
To be a platform she controls — first for her own work, and as a foundation she can build outward from. Founding a label is a statement of intent: it signals that the artist intends to shape her catalog and her release strategy herself rather than hand them off. For an independent artist in a crowded city, that ownership is leverage, and it is the kind of move that turns a promising DJ into a self-sustaining operation.
THE SCENE
How does she connect Los Angeles to the wider dance world?
By carrying the LA underground onto international stages and bringing that perspective home. Playing Sound in Los Angeles, KitKat Club in Berlin and Ultra in Miami within the same arc means ZLATA moves between the local warehouse circuit and the global festival and club network — and that mobility makes her a kind of ambassador. The energy she develops in LA rooms travels with her, and the lessons of Berlin and Miami flow back into the sets she plays at home.
Why does a platform like KEEPITIL matter for an artist like this?
Because independent, self-built artists are exactly the ones a California underground directory exists to surface. ZLATA's story — classical roots, a self-founded label, a refusal to be packaged — is the kind of narrative that gets flattened in mainstream coverage organized around hype cycles. KEEPITIL provides shared context that ties her to the broader OC and LA underground, helping fans who follow the scene discover an LA artist building something durable rather than disposable.
What is next?
More releases through debosh, more original material that puts her voice and production front and center, and continued movement between the LA underground and the international circuit. The ambition is not a single breakout but a body of work — the steady accumulation of records, sets and shows that comes from an artist who decided early to build the whole thing herself.
