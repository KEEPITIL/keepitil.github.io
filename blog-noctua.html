NOCTUA

Dark Techno from the Heart of Los Angeles

📍 Los Angeles, CA 🎵 Techno / Dark Techno / Industrial ⭐ KEEPITIL Artist

NOCTUA has been carving out space in the Los Angeles techno underground since 2021 with sets that draw on industrial, dark techno, and EBM — music built for rooms where the lights stay off and the kick drum does the talking. A regular at DTLA warehouse events and a rising presence on the Factory 93 circuit, NOCTUA represents the serious, uncompromising end of the LA techno spectrum.

"Techno is about patience, not performance — the best sets are the ones where you don't notice the DJ."

THE STORY
The music you can't hear anywhere but a dark room at 2AM draws you in — what was that gateway?
Listening to a recording of a Berghain set from 2015 at home through headphones. The recording quality was terrible, the room sound was cavernous, and somehow that made it more real than anything I'd heard at a produced event. The music had this patience to it — it wasn't trying to impress you, it just existed and dared you to match its energy. That was the sound I wanted to play.
You work across industrial, EBM, and dark techno — where do those connect for you?
They all share the same DNA: music made with machinery, about machinery, that creates something uncomfortably human. EBM has the political energy, industrial has the texture and aggression, dark techno has the hypnotic structure. When I'm sequencing a set, I think of them as different temperatures of the same fire.

THE CRAFT
How do you read a warehouse crowd differently from a club crowd?
Warehouse crowds self-select. Someone who drove 45 minutes to an unlisted address in an industrial park has already committed — they're not there to be convinced, they're there to go. Club crowds are more mixed; you're constantly earning their attention. I love both challenges, but warehouses let me take risks that a club environment punishes.
What's your production process like outside of DJing?
I work almost entirely in hardware — Elektron sequencers, analog synthesizers, drum machines that cost more than they should. The process is slow and physical, which is deliberate. I want to feel resistance when I'm making music, because the music I want to make shouldn't be easy to produce.
